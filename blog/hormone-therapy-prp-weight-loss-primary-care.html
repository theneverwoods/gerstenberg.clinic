Hormone Therapy, PRP, Weight Loss, or Primary Care: Where to Start

How to know which service is the right starting point for you, and why a full primary care workup usually comes first.

By gerstenberg.clinicSeptember 21, 20269 min read

Fatigue that doesn’t lift after a full night of sleep, a body that stopped responding the way it used to, or intimacy that’s quietly become a source of stress rather than connection: these aren’t complaints you can fix with more willpower. They’re signals that something physiological has shifted, and the question isn’t whether to address it. It’s where to start.

Hormone therapy, PRP treatments, medical weight loss and primary care each address real problems. But they’re not interchangeable, and starting with the wrong one wastes time you don’t have.

The short answer

If you’re deciding between hormone therapy, PRP, medical weight loss or primary care, start with a primary care evaluation. A comprehensive evaluation by your primary care provider looks at all the possible causes of your symptoms, rules out underlying conditions, and determines which specialized treatment will actually work for your body. Jumping straight to a specialized treatment without that foundation often means treating a symptom while the root cause goes undiagnosed.

Key takeaways

  • A full primary care baseline isn’t a formality. It’s the diagnostic foundation that tells you which specialized treatment your body actually needs.
  • Hormone therapy (such as BioTE) delivers consistent, bioidentical hormone levels through pellet implants, not synthetic spikes and crashes.
  • PRP therapy uses your own platelet-rich plasma to stimulate tissue repair, so results depend on your body’s healing capacity rather than a drug’s potency.
  • Medical weight loss addresses the metabolic and hormonal reasons weight won’t move, not just calorie math.
  • We offer all of these pathways under one roof, so your treatments are coordinated, not siloed.

Why does starting in the wrong place cost you more than time?

Most people who’ve been dealing with chronic fatigue, unexplained weight gain or declining sexual function don’t arrive at a clinic with a clear diagnosis. They arrive with a symptom and a guess.

The guess is usually wrong. Not because the person is uninformed, but because these conditions share overlapping presentations. Low testosterone in men looks like depression and fatigue. Perimenopause in women looks like anxiety and insomnia. Thyroid dysfunction looks like both. Insulin resistance looks like all of them.

When you treat the guess rather than the diagnosis, you can spend months on a treatment that addresses the wrong system entirely.

Specialized treatments are calibrated to correct a known imbalance. Without knowing what’s actually imbalanced, you’re adjusting a dial without a gauge.

The “treatment entry point” problem

The treatment entry point problem is the pattern in which patients choose their first treatment based on the symptom they find most distressing, rather than on the underlying cause driving all their symptoms.

It’s not a knowledge failure. It’s a framing failure. When you’re exhausted and gaining weight, “weight loss program” feels like the obvious answer. When your libido has dropped, “intimacy wellness” sounds right. When your joints ache, “PRP” makes sense. But these treatments work best when they’re selected after a full picture is established, not before.

Consider a common scenario: a woman in her mid-forties starts a medical weight loss program because she’s gained 15 pounds despite no change in her diet or activity. The program helps, but progress stalls. Six months later, a hormone panel reveals she’s been in perimenopause the entire time, with estrogen and progesterone levels that were actively working against her metabolism. The weight loss program wasn’t wrong. It was just incomplete without the hormonal context.

That’s why our approach begins with primary care. Not as a gatekeeping step, but as a diagnostic foundation that makes every treatment that follows more precise.

The four pathways: what each one does and when it’s the right call

Primary care

Primary care is the baseline evaluation and ongoing management of your overall health, including labs, physical exams, chronic disease management and referral coordination.

It’s the right starting point for almost everyone. Not because it’s the most exciting option, but because it’s the one that tells you which of the other options you actually need. If you haven’t had a recent comprehensive panel that includes thyroid, sex hormones, metabolic markers and inflammatory indicators, you’re making treatment decisions without the data needed to make them accurately.

Hormone therapy (BioTE)

BioTE hormone therapy is a bioidentical hormone replacement method that uses small pellets placed under the skin to deliver consistent hormone levels over several months.

The key difference from conventional HRT is the delivery method. Oral or topical hormones create peaks and valleys in blood levels. Pellets release steadily, which more closely mirrors how your body produced hormones before levels declined.

This matters because many of the symptoms people attribute to “getting older” are actually symptoms of hormonal insufficiency: brain fog, low energy, poor sleep, reduced muscle mass, weight gain and diminished libido. Correcting the insufficiency doesn’t reverse aging. It removes a physiological drag that was making everything harder.

BioTE is appropriate when labs confirm a hormonal deficit and primary care has ruled out other causes for the same symptoms.

PRP therapy

PRP (platelet-rich plasma) therapy concentrates growth factors from your own blood and reintroduces them into a targeted area to stimulate tissue regeneration and repair.

Because PRP uses your own biology, it doesn’t introduce foreign substances. The mechanism is activation, not replacement: your platelets contain growth factors that signal repair, and concentrating them in one area accelerates a process your body already knows how to do.

We use PRP for knee and joint healing and for intimacy wellness, including the O-Shot® for women and the P-Shot® for men. For sexual health specifically, it can address tissue sensitivity and function in ways that pharmaceutical options don’t.

The honest limitation: PRP results depend on your body’s healing response. Patients with significant systemic inflammation, uncontrolled blood sugar or severe hormonal deficiency often see slower or less pronounced results until those underlying conditions are addressed.

Medical weight loss

Medical weight loss is a clinically supervised approach to changing body composition that accounts for metabolic rate, hormonal status and individual physiology, rather than relying on calorie restriction alone.

Conventional dieting fails most people not for lack of discipline, but because the advice ignores the metabolic and hormonal variables that determine how your body stores and burns fat. Insulin resistance, cortisol dysregulation, low thyroid function and sex hormone imbalance all shift the equation in ways no amount of calorie counting can correct. Our program addresses those variables directly, which is why it works in cases where self-directed approaches have repeatedly failed.

How to know which pathway fits your situation

Use this as a starting orientation, not a self-diagnosis. Its core principle: when symptoms overlap, start with the pathway that yields the most diagnostic information. That’s almost always primary care.

Who this approach is not for

Our model works best for people who are ready to treat the actual cause, not just manage the most annoying symptom.

If you’re looking for a single quick fix with no follow-up, coordinated care will feel like more than you signed up for. These treatments are designed to work together over time, not as isolated one-time interventions. Patients who skip the diagnostic phase and jump straight to a specific treatment sometimes find it underperforms because the foundational variables weren’t assessed first. Our approach asks for a bit more patience up front in exchange for results that actually hold.

The money-back guarantee on our intimacy wellness services reflects that confidence: when the right diagnostic groundwork is in place, the treatments perform.

What happens after you start?

The most common question after starting any of these treatments is: how long before I notice a difference?

For BioTE hormone therapy, most patients report changes in energy and mood within two to three weeks of their first pellet insertion, with fuller effects over the following two to three months as levels stabilize. This timeline varies with how depleted your baseline was and how your body absorbs the pellet.

For PRP, the repair process is biological, so it follows your body’s own timeline. Visible changes typically begin within four to eight weeks, with continued improvement over several months. We can also add exosomes, the packets of growth factors that “supercharge” your PRP, to augment the effect.

The second question people ask: do I have to stay on this forever? For hormone therapy, the honest answer is that the underlying cause of the deficiency, age-related hormonal decline, doesn’t reverse. Maintenance is ongoing, but dosing is adjusted over time based on repeat lab results. It’s not a lifetime commitment to a fixed protocol. It’s an evolving one, which is why the primary care relationship matters even after you’ve started a specialized treatment.

Common questions

How do I know if my fatigue is hormonal or something else entirely?

You don’t, without labs. Fatigue is one of the least specific symptoms in medicine. It can come from thyroid dysfunction, sex hormone decline, anemia, sleep apnea, blood sugar dysregulation, neuroendocrine dysfunction, infections, or a combination of several. A comprehensive panel through a primary care provider is the only way to isolate the actual drivers rather than treating the most plausible guess.

Is BioTE safe for women who’ve heard conflicting things about hormone replacement therapy?

The concerns most people have heard about HRT are largely tied to synthetic progestins and conjugated equine estrogens studied in the early 2000s. BioTE uses bioidentical hormones, meaning the molecular structure matches what your body produces. The risk profile is different, and steady-release pellets avoid the blood-level spikes associated with older oral formulations. Your evaluation includes your personal and family history before any treatment is recommended.

Can PRP really help with sexual function?

PRP’s mechanism in sexual wellness is tissue regeneration, improved nerve sensation and increased blood flow, not a psychological or placebo effect. In women, it’s used to address sensitivity and arousal by stimulating tissue in the treated area. In men, it supports erectile function through similar vascular and tissue mechanisms. Results depend on your baseline health and the specific issue being treated, which is why a consultation to assess fit matters before committing.

What makes medical weight loss different from following a diet plan?

A diet plan addresses input and output. Medical weight loss addresses why your body isn’t responding to input and output the way it should. If you have insulin resistance, your cells aren’t using glucose efficiently. If your cortisol is chronically elevated, your body stores fat in response to stress. If your thyroid is underactive, your metabolic rate is slower than standard formulas assume. None of these is fixed by eating less.

Do I need a referral?

No referral is required. You can schedule a consultation directly, and your primary care evaluation is the starting point that determines which treatments are appropriate for you.

How often do hormone pellets need to be replaced?

For women, BioTE pellets are typically replaced every three to four months. For men, the interval is usually four to six months. Timing is based on follow-up labs that measure your hormone levels as the pellet depletes, so the schedule is adjusted to your individual absorption rate rather than a fixed calendar.

What if I’ve already tried one of these treatments elsewhere without results?

The most common reason a treatment underperforms is that it was chosen without a full diagnostic picture. If you had PRP without addressing an underlying hormonal deficiency, or started hormone therapy without proper baseline labs, the treatment may have been working against an unresolved variable. We start by understanding what was and wasn’t assessed before, so your next step is informed by your full history, not just your most recent attempt.

The bottom line

The body doesn’t fail all at once. It shifts gradually, in ways that are easy to rationalize until they’re impossible to ignore. The treatments exist. The question is whether you’re using the right one, and a primary care evaluation is the fastest way to find out.
