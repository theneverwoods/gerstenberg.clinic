The basics

What is peripheral neuropathy?

Peripheral neuropathy is nerve damage that causes pain, most often in the feet and hands.

It can feel like burning, tingling, numbness or “pins and needles.” Over time it can make it hard to stand, walk or hold things for long, and numb feet make falls and unnoticed injuries more likely.

Diabetes is the most common cause, but chemotherapy, dialysis, poor circulation, vitamin deficiencies and other conditions can damage nerves too.

There isn’t a cure for neuropathy yet. But there are treatments that can slow it down, ease the symptoms and often partly reverse its effects.

Damaged nerves need two things to recover: blood flow to deliver oxygen and nutrients, and stimulation to retrain them to fire correctly. Our program targets both, in the clinic and at home, and the treatments work best when they’re used together, consistently.

In the clinic

Three in-clinic treatments for nerve pain

Your provider builds your plan from three in-clinic therapies, depending on what’s causing your neuropathy and how severe it is. They work on different parts of the problem: calming pain and inflammation, protecting nerves from oxidative damage, and fixing the metabolism problems that damage nerves in the first place.

01

MLS laser therapy

Our MLS laser uses two synchronized wavelengths of light to reduce pain and inflammation and support nerve healing. Sessions are short, painless and drug-free, with no downtime. In published case reports, a patient with years of nerve pain in the jaw and face was pain-free and off all medication within 13 weeks and still pain-free 2 years later. More on MLS laser therapy

02

IV alpha-lipoic acid

Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA) is a powerful antioxidant that protects nerves from the oxidative stress behind much of diabetic neuropathy. Given by IV, it reaches levels that pills can’t. In clinical trials, IV ALA eased burning, stabbing pain and numbness within 3 weeks, with improvement first noticed after about 8 days. More on IV therapy

03

Microdose infusion (MDI)

For patients with diabetes or prediabetes, MDI delivers tiny, precisely timed doses of insulin by IV to mimic a healthy pancreas. That helps the body process carbohydrates properly, lowering the inflammation that damages nerves. After about three months, patients may notice less fatigue and feeling returning to their feet. More on MDI

Our 90-day at-home program for peripheral neuropathy

A take-home kit designed specifically for peripheral neuropathy in the feet and legs. You use it every day for about 90 days, so your nerves get consistent treatment between clinic visits.

Neurostimulation

Gentle electrical stimulation retrains damaged nerve endings to fire correctly, blocks pain signals and improves circulation in the skin. In studies, most patients reported less pain, and some became symptom-free.

Red & infrared light therapy

Red and near-infrared light releases nitric oxide, which widens small blood vessels and brings more oxygen to starving nerves. Studies link it to restored foot sensation, less pain and better balance.

Vibration therapy

A vibration platform under your feet boosts blood flow to the legs and stimulates the sensors that help with balance. In studies, foot vibration raised calf blood flow by up to 50%.

Short daily sessions

Each part of the routine takes minutes and fits into your day at home. Consistency matters more than intensity.

Guided by our team

We set up your plan, show you how to use everything and check your progress along the way.

Then, maintenance

After 90 days, most patients can scale back to a maintenance routine. Diet, exercise and supplements stay part of the long-term plan.

Is the at-home kit only for peripheral neuropathy?

Yes. The 90-day at-home program is designed for peripheral neuropathy in the feet and legs. If your nerve pain is in your neck, back, arms or face, our in-clinic treatments such as MLS laser therapy, IV alpha-lipoic acid and spinal decompression may still help, and we’ll recommend what fits.

Do I need to have diabetes?

No. Diabetes is the most common cause of neuropathy, but we also treat neuropathy from chemotherapy, dialysis, poor circulation and other causes. Microdose infusion (MDI) is mainly for patients with diabetes or prediabetes.

Can neuropathy be cured?

There isn’t a cure yet. The goal is to slow it down, ease your symptoms and, often, partly reverse its effects, such as getting feeling back in your feet. Results vary from person to person.

How soon will I notice a difference?

It varies. In studies, some patients noticed better sensation within 6 to 12 light-therapy sessions, and IV alpha-lipoic acid improvements started after about 8 days. Nerves heal slowly, so we plan for about 90 days of consistent treatment.

Do the treatments hurt?

No. MLS laser therapy is painless, neurostimulation feels like a light tingle, and light and vibration therapy are relaxing. IV treatments involve a small needle for the IV line.

Is it safe for everyone?

Tell us if you have a pacemaker or another implanted electrical device, are pregnant, or have open wounds on your feet. Some parts of the program may not be right for you, and we screen for this at your consultation.

Should I stop my neuropathy medication?

No. Keep taking your medications unless your provider tells you otherwise. If your symptoms improve, we’ll talk with you about whether any changes make sense.

How much does it cost?

Pricing and packages vary from patient to patient. It’s not one-size-fits-all: your provider will recommend a plan after your consultation, and we’ll go over pricing with you before you commit to anything. Cherry payment plans are available.
