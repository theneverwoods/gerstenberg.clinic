The basics

What is spinal decompression?

When a bulging or herniated disc presses on a nerve, the pain can travel well beyond your spine: down a leg, or into a shoulder and arm.

Non-surgical spinal decompression is a gentle, computer-controlled stretch of the spine. You lie comfortably on a padded table while it slowly pulls, holds and releases in cycles, taking pressure off the discs and joints between your vertebrae. There are no needles, no medication and no incisions, and you stay fully clothed.

We use the Hill DT, an FDA-cleared decompression table made by Hill Laboratories in Pennsylvania. It treats both the low back and the neck.

How can stretching the spine help?

Your spinal discs are cushions with a tough outer ring and a gel-like center. With wear or injury, that center can bulge or push through the ring and press on nearby nerves. Discs also have very little blood supply, so they heal slowly.

When the spine is gently stretched and relaxed in cycles, pressure inside the disc drops. One study measured pressure inside the disc falling below −100 mmHg during decompression. That lower pressure is thought to help draw bulging material back toward the center of the disc and let water, oxygen and nutrients move in to support healing.

In a 2022 randomized trial, follow-up MRI scans showed herniated discs shrank more after decompression than after a sham treatment.

How it works

What makes the Hill DT different?

Older traction tables pull with one steady force, and the body tends to fight back: the muscles around the spine tighten up, which is called guarding. That can limit how much the discs actually benefit.

The Hill DT is built to avoid that. Its sensors take more than 10,000 readings for every millimeter of movement and ease off as your body nears its comfort threshold. The table then cycles between a gentle pull and a partial release, so your muscles stay relaxed while the discs are decompressed.

Your provider chooses from eight treatment programs and sets the force and angle for you. The table saves your settings, so each visit picks up where the last one left off.

01

Cycles, not a constant pull

The table pulls, holds and partially releases in timed cycles, giving your muscles a chance to relax between stretches.

02

Sensors that prevent guarding

Load sensors read the pull continuously and back off before your body tenses, for a smooth, low-force treatment.

03

Angle targets the disc

The table tilts up to 25° for the low back and 15° for the neck. Lower angles focus on the lowest discs; higher angles reach discs further up.

04

Gentle force range

Low-back treatment ranges from 5 to 100 lb and neck treatment from 5 to 35 lb. Your provider starts low and adjusts as you go.

05

Neck cradle

For cervical treatment, a padded cradle supports the base of your skull and can tilt to take pressure off the back of the disc.

06

Built-in safety

A patient stop switch and a quick-release strap mean you can end treatment at any time; the table slowly returns to its starting position.

Your visit

What a treatment looks like

  1. Consultation & exam

    We review your history, symptoms and any imaging, and screen for anything that would make decompression unsafe.

  2. Get comfortable

    You lie on your back, fully clothed. For the low back, a harness fits around your lower ribs and hips and your calves rest on a cushion. For the neck, a padded cradle supports your head.

  3. Relax for about 30 minutes

    The table gently pulls and releases. You'll feel a stretch, not pain, and you hold the stop switch the whole time.

  4. Head back to your day

    There's no downtime. We may pair sessions with heat, MLS laser or simple core exercises to support your results.

What is non-surgical spinal decompression?

It's a gentle, motorized stretch of the spine done on a specialized table. The table slowly pulls, holds and releases in cycles to take pressure off the discs and joints in your low back or neck. It's done without surgery, needles or medication.

How is decompression different from traction or an inversion table?

Traditional traction applies one steady pull, and inversion tables use gravity by tipping you upside down. A decompression table like the Hill DT is computer-controlled: it pulls, holds and partially releases in cycles, and its sensors ease off before your muscles tense.

To be straightforward, some studies found results similar to conventional motorized traction. We see decompression as a comfortable, precisely controlled way to deliver this kind of care, not a miracle cure. Inversion tables also carry risks for people with high blood pressure, heart disease, glaucoma or pregnancy.

Does spinal decompression hurt?

It shouldn't. You'll feel a gentle stretch in your back or neck, and many patients find it relaxing. Some soreness afterward is possible. Tell us right away if anything feels painful, and remember you can stop the table at any time with the switch in your hand.

What happens during a session?

You lie on your back, fully clothed. For low-back treatment, a harness fits around your lower ribs and hips and your calves rest on a padded support, which flattens your lower back. For neck treatment, a padded cradle supports your head. The table then runs your program while you rest.

How long is each session?

About 30 minutes on the table. Plan a little extra time if we add heat, laser or exercises to your visit.

How many sessions will I need?

Our treatment plans start at a minimum of 20 to 24 sessions. Your provider may recommend more based on your condition and how you respond, and will go over your plan at your consultation.

When will I notice results?

Decompression usually works gradually. Many patients don't notice much change for the first several sessions, and improvement often shows up around weeks 3 to 4. That's why completing the full course matters.

Am I a good candidate?

Decompression is used for back or neck pain from disc problems and irritated nerves, such as bulging or herniated discs, sciatica, degenerative disc disease and pinched nerves. It's often a good fit if rest, medication or physical therapy haven't given you enough relief and you want to avoid or delay surgery. We'll examine you and review any imaging to decide together.

Who should not have spinal decompression?

It isn't used during pregnancy, for anyone under 18, or for people with osteoporosis, a vertebral fracture, spondylolysis or spondylolisthesis, spinal fusion, artificial discs or other spinal hardware, a spinal tumor or spreading cancer, an abdominal aortic aneurysm, some congenital spine abnormalities, or body weight above the table's limit. We screen for all of these at your consultation.

Can decompression help sciatica?

Sciatica caused by a disc pressing on a nerve root is one of the most common reasons people try decompression. In a 2022 randomized trial of patients with lumbar radiculopathy, adding decompression to physical therapy reduced pain and disability more than therapy alone. Sciatica caused by pregnancy or a tumor is not treated with decompression.

Can it help a herniated or bulging disc?

Disc problems are what decompression is designed for. In a 2022 sham-controlled trial, follow-up MRI scans showed herniated discs shrank an average of 27.6% after decompression, compared with 7.1% after the sham treatment.

Can it help neck pain?

Yes. The Hill DT has a cervical setting with a padded head cradle and a gentler force range for neck and arm pain from cervical discs. Research on neck decompression is more limited than for the low back, though reviews of cervical traction have found short-term pain relief.

Is it safe? Are there side effects?

Decompression is non-invasive, and the clinical trials we cite reported no serious complications. Some people have temporary soreness or a short-term increase in pain as the spine adjusts. Let your provider know how you feel after each session so we can adjust your settings. We screen carefully for conditions where decompression shouldn't be used.

Is spinal decompression covered by insurance?

Usually not. Most insurance plans, including Medicare, don't cover non-surgical decompression. We offer Cherry payment plans and also accept CareCredit, and we'll go over your options at your consultation.

How much does treatment cost?

Pricing and packages vary from patient to patient. It’s not one-size-fits-all: your provider will recommend a plan after your consultation, and we’ll go over pricing with you before you commit to anything.

Can decompression be combined with other treatments?

Yes. Decompression tends to work best as part of a fuller plan that can include heat, MLS laser therapy, posture work and core strengthening, and nutrition guidance. Your provider will recommend what makes sense for you.

What should I wear?

Wear comfortable clothing you can lie down in. You stay fully clothed for treatment.

Is the Hill DT FDA-cleared?

Yes. The Hill DT is an FDA-cleared Class II powered traction table, listed in the FDA device database.
